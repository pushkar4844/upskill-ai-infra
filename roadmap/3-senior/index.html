About this phase

Phase 3 changes the kind of work. Instead of running tools, you produce what a solutions architect owns: designs, runbooks, automation and decisions that other people will rely on. Most labs here are free because the output is a document or code, not a measurement.

You write the acceptance runbook a customer signs before taking a cluster, design a 512-GPU network on paper, build automation that cordons and repairs bad nodes without draining the whole cluster, write a Kubernetes operator, and plan a 1,024-GPU rollout. Each lab draws on numbers and skills from Phases 1 and 2.

This is also where you practise communication: decision memos, executive status updates and reliability reports. Senior interviews test judgement and clarity as much as technical depth, and these labs give you written examples to talk through.

By the end you can

  • A cluster acceptance runbook with thresholds and a go/no-go report
  • A 512-GPU rail-optimised fabric design with bill of materials
  • Bad-node bisection and automated cordon/drain with a circuit breaker
  • A Kubebuilder operator and a security policy pack for GPU clusters
  • A 1,024-GPU rollout plan, a storage tiering recommendation and an executive status template

How the phase runs

  1. Prove a cluster

    Acceptance runbook (B5), firmware baseline (C4), fleet BMC monitoring (D3).

  2. Design

    512-GPU fabric (E4), bad-node bisection (F4), Slurm vs Kubernetes memo (G4).

  3. Automate

    Node remediation (H6), an operator (H7), a security policy pack (H8).

  4. Plan and communicate

    1,024-GPU rollout (I2), storage tiers (I3), reliability analytics (J2), executive updates (J3).

Tips

  • Ask someone to review your runbook and fabric design, then answer their questions in writing.
  • Use real numbers from your own Phase 2 results wherever a design needs a threshold.
  • Keep each document to what a busy reader needs: decision, numbers, risks, next step.
