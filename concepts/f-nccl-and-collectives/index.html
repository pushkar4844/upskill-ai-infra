Concept primer

NCCL and collectives

NCCL is the library that moves data between GPUs. Its benchmark numbers are the clearest health signal for a training cluster, if you know how to read them.

Key ideas

  1. Collectives: all-reduce (sum gradients across ranks), all-gather, reduce-scatter, all-to-all (expert parallelism), broadcast.
  2. algbw = bytes / time. busbw corrects for the algorithm so it is comparable to link bandwidth whatever the rank count: all-reduce x 2(n-1)/n, all-gather and reduce-scatter x (n-1)/n.
  3. Ring algorithms suit large messages; tree algorithms cut latency at scale. NCCL picks automatically, and NCCL_ALGO lets you compare.
  4. NCCL_DEBUG=INFO shows which transport and NIC it chose (NVLink, P2P, NET/IB, NET/OFI for EFA, Socket). Always check it before trusting a number.
  5. Collectives move at the speed of the slowest rank: one straggler slows everyone, one dead rank hangs everyone until a timeout fires.

Common mistakes

  • Quoting small-message busbw as the cluster's bandwidth
  • Benchmarking without checking the transport in NCCL_DEBUG
  • No timeout, so a dead rank hangs a job for hours

What interviewers probe

algbw vs busbw: why does busbw exist? busbw drops going from 2 to 4 nodes: where do you look?

Terms

algbw / busbw
nccl-tests bandwidth figures. algbw = bytes/time; busbw applies an algorithm factor (all-reduce 2(n-1)/n) so it compares with link bandwidth.
All-reduce
Collective that sums (or otherwise reduces) a buffer across all ranks and gives every rank the result. Used for gradients in data-parallel training.
Collective
A communication operation involving a group of ranks: all-reduce, all-gather, reduce-scatter, all-to-all, broadcast.
NCCL
NVIDIA Collective Communications Library: GPU-to-GPU collectives over NVLink, PCIe and networks.
Straggler
A rank slower than the others; collectives wait for it, so it slows the whole job.
