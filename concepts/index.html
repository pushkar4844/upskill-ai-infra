Concepts

Twelve topics, five minutes each

Each primer gives you the key ideas, the common mistakes and what interviewers probe, then links to the labs where you practise it. Read one before a topic's first lab.

A

Linux and containers

Every GPU problem shows up first as a Linux symptom: a slow node, a missing device, a stuck process. You need a fixed order of checks and a clear picture of how a GPU reaches a container.

4 labs · phases 1, 2B

GPU health and diagnostics

GPU health is read from three places: kernel XID errors, ECC and row-remapping counters, and active diagnostics (DCGM, burn-in). Fleets combine passive monitoring with scheduled active tests.

5 labs · phases 1, 2, 3C

Server hardware and topology

Performance depends on where things sit: which CPU socket, which PCIe switch, which NIC. Topology decides whether data takes the short path or crosses the system.

4 labs · phases 1, 2, 3D

BMC and out-of-band

The BMC is a small independent computer on the server board. It lets you see and control the machine when the OS is down, which is most of early bring-up.

3 labs · phases 1, 2, 3E

RDMA and interconnect

Training moves huge buffers between GPUs every step. RDMA fabrics (InfiniBand, RoCE, AWS EFA) do it without CPU copies; their design and congestion control decide how well jobs scale.

5 labs · phases 1, 2, 3F

NCCL and collectives

NCCL is the library that moves data between GPUs. Its benchmark numbers are the clearest health signal for a training cluster, if you know how to read them.

4 labs · phases 1, 2, 3G

Slurm and provisioning

Most large training clusters run Slurm; many companies run Kubernetes or both. You need to operate Slurm, keep bad nodes out of the queue and know how servers become nodes in the first place.

5 labs · phases 1, 2, 3H

Kubernetes for GPUs

On Kubernetes, GPUs arrive through operators and device APIs. The hard parts are sharing GPUs, scheduling whole jobs at once, giving pods RDMA, and remediating bad nodes safely.

8 labs · phases 1, 2, 3I

Cloud, capacity and storage

Customers rent GPUs from clouds. Knowing what each cloud really gives you (NICs per GPU, fabric, placement, quotas, storage) is what separates a cloud-aware architect from a hardware specialist.

3 labs · phases 2, 3J

Observability and reporting

Observability for GPU fleets means the right metrics, the right alerts and the ability to turn failure data into decisions.

3 labs · phases 2, 3K

LLM and training literacy

You don't need to train models, but you must do the arithmetic and speak the workload's language: memory, compute, parallelism and serving metrics.

4 labs · phases 1, 2L

Capstone and proof

The capstone turns 48 labs into one story you can tell in ten minutes, backed by a public repository.

2 labs · phases 4
